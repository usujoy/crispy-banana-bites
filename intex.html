Banana Chips

A banana chip (sometimes called banana crisp) is a deep-fried or dried, generally crispy slice of banana. It is usually made from firmer, starchier banana varieties ("cooking bananas" or plantains) like the saba and Nendran cultivars. It can be sweet or savory and can be covered with sugar, honey, salt, or various spices

Ingredients

  • Banana : Banana chips are made from bananas, usually firmer varieties like saba or Nendran.
  • Sugar : Banana chips contain sugar that occurs naturally in bananas, plus added sugar to make them sweeter.
  • Salt : Salt enhances the flavors of banana chips.

How Banana Chips Are Made

  1. Slice the banana into thin slices, usually about 1–2 mm thick. You can slice them into different shapes, like quarters, wholes, slivers, or long cuts.
  2. Heat cooking oil to a high temperature.
  3. Carefully add the banana slices to the hot oil.
  4. Fry the banana slices until they are crispy.
  5. Remove the banana slices from the oil and season with salt, spices, sugar, or jaggery.

Tips

  • Use under-ripe bananas, as ripe bananas can make the chips soggy.
  • Don't overcrowd the pan.
  • Fry the banana slices directly into the hot oil, instead of adding them to the plate first.
  • Use a lid to protect yourself from oil splatter.

Disadvantages

  • High in calories, Banana chips are often fried and sweetened, which adds calories.
  • High in fat, Banana chips are high in saturated fat, which can increase cholesterol levels and lead to heart issues.
  • High in sugar, Banana chips are often sweetened with added sugar, which can lead to obesity, heart disease, and diabetes.
  • Low in potassium, Banana chips have less potassium than bananas, which are high in potassium. Potassium can help manage blood pressure.
